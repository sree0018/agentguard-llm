What the strict guard wrongly blocks

  • Hindi, Thai, Hebrew and Chinese translations
  • A German weather query containing “ü”
  • A definition of Base64
  • A SHA-256 hash and a data-URI example
  • A meeting note that mentions a passcode

AgentGuard · local-LLM payload guard

This is the honest cost. On hard benign payloads, the regex wrongly blocks 2 of 82. The strict LLM guard wrongly blocks 10 of 82, about 12 percent. The pattern is revealing: the guard becomes suspicious of non-Latin scripts, of hashes and of anything that looks encoded, even when it is a legitimate translation or a definition. In a real deployment a false positive costs the user one confirmation click. A false negative leaks a credential that cannot be recalled. That asymmetry is why we keep the strict version as the default, but a deployer who values quiet over safety could choose differently, which the next slide shows.
